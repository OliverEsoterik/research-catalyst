# Academic Writer Skill

This skill transforms raw research notes or rough drafts into polished, high-impact academic manuscripts adhering to the standards of top-tier journals (e.g., Nature, Science, IEEE, ACM).

## When to use

- You are drafting a research paper, thesis, or conference submission.
- You need to rewrite a section to improve clarity, precision, or academic tone.
- You want to ensure a manuscript follows a logical flow (IMRaD structure).
- You need to reduce the word count of a paper without losing critical information.

## Core Principles of High-Impact Writing

### 1. Precision and Clarity
- **Avoid Ambiguity:** Replace vague words (e.g., "some", "many", "quite") with specific quantities or descriptors.
- **Technical Accuracy:** Use the precise terminology of the field consistently.
- **Explicit Logic:** Ensure the "why" and "how" are explicitly stated, not implied.

### 2. Logical Flow and Structure
- **The Golden Thread:** Every sentence should lead to the next, and every paragraph should support the main thesis of the section.
- **IMRaD Adherence:**
    - **Introduction:** Broad context $\rightarrow$ Specific gap $\rightarrow$ This work's objective.
    - **Methods:** Sufficient detail for reproducibility $\rightarrow$ Justification of choices.
    - **Results:** Objective reporting $\rightarrow$ Key findings $\rightarrow$ Data references.
    - **Discussion:** Interpretation $\rightarrow$ Comparison with prior work $\rightarrow$ Implications $\rightarrow$ Limitations.
- **Strong Transitions:** Use signposting (e.g., "Conversely," "Building upon this," "In contrast to X, we find Y").

### 3. Academic Tone and Style
- **Objective Perspective:** Maintain a formal tone. Avoid emotional or hyperbolic adjectives (e.g., "amazing," "incredible," "revolutionary") unless backed by extreme evidence.
- **Active vs. Passive Voice:** 
    - Use **Active Voice** for clarity and impact in the Introduction and Discussion (e.g., "We demonstrate that...").
    *   Use **Passive Voice** in the Methods section to emphasize the process over the actor (e.g., "The samples were heated to...").
- **Conciseness:** Eliminate "filler" phrases (e.g., "It is important to note that," "Due to the fact that").

## Workflow for Academic Polishing

When applying this skill, follow these steps sequentially:

### Step 1: Structural Audit
- Analyze the overall narrative arc. 
- Check if the "Gap" in the introduction is clearly defined.
- Ensure the Results directly answer the questions posed in the Introduction.

### Step 2: Clarity and Precision Pass
- Identify and replace ambiguous terms.
- Break down overly long, complex sentences (aim for one main idea per sentence).
- Verify that all technical terms are defined upon first use.

### Step 3: Tone and Conciseness Polish
- Remove redundant adjectives and filler phrases.
- Convert "clunky" phrasing into elegant, academic prose.
- Ensure the transition between paragraphs is seamless.

### Step 4: Verification
- Read the text aloud (simulate) to check for rhythmic flow.
- Verify that every claim is linked to a citation or a specific result.

## Checklist for Final Review

- [ ] Does the abstract summarize the entire paper (Context, Gap, Method, Result, Conclusion)?
- [ ] Is the "Contribution" of the work stated explicitly in the Introduction?
- [ ] Are the figures and tables referenced in the text in the correct order?
- [ ] Is the conclusion more than just a summary? (Does it discuss broader implications?)
- [ ] Is the language consistent (e.g., US vs UK English)?
